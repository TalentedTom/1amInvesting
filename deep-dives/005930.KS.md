# 1. Core Thesis

Samsung has four strategic advantages.

## 1.1 Memory oligopoly scale

Samsung remains one of the three structural global memory suppliers. DRAM, HBM, NAND, enterprise SSD, CXL memory, and future HBM generations all benefit from the same underlying fact:

> AI inference and training are memory constrained.

Samsung is not the purest HBM leader — SK Hynix remains the cleanest quality leader in HBM today — but Samsung has broader exposure across DRAM, NAND, HBM, foundry and packaging.

## 1.2 HBM4 / HBM4E recovery

Samsung officially began shipping 12-layer HBM4E samples to global customers in May 2026. The product uses Samsung’s 1c DRAM process and 4nm logic base die, supports speeds up to 16Gbps per pin, and is positioned for next-generation AI workloads.

The new Korean internal-briefing breadcrumb says HBM4E reliability-test yield has moved above 70%. Industry mature yield is often viewed around 80%+, so this suggests the product may be moving into a more stable development range if accurate.

## 1.3 D1d / HBM5 optionality

The same Korean report says Samsung’s D1d DRAM process is targeting PRA in November. D1d matters because Samsung plans to apply it to future HBM5 and later HBM generations.

D1d success would improve Samsung’s probability of regaining leadership in the post-HBM4E generation.

## 1.4 Foundry / packaging / TPU optionality

Google is reportedly evaluating Samsung for part of a next-generation TPU memory I/O die on Samsung 2nm. That does not make Samsung the primary TPU foundry today, but it validates the strategic direction:

> Custom AI silicon customers want non-TSMC optionality, and Samsung is the only company that can credibly bundle HBM, memory-interface logic, foundry, and packaging.

Samsung announced that it began shipping industry-first 12-layer HBM4E samples. Samsung said HBM4E achieves up to 16Gbps, improves speed by more than 20% versus HBM4, and uses proven HBM4 production experience and technology enhancements.

Source: Samsung Semiconductor, “Samsung Electronics Begins Shipment of Industry-First HBM4E Samples,” May 29, 2026.

## 2.2 Reuters HBM4E confirmation

Reuters reported that Samsung had started shipping faster 12-layer HBM4E chip samples to customers, using 1c DRAM and a 4nm logic base die, and that the event was viewed as Samsung pushing to regain share in the AI memory market.

Source: Reuters, “Samsung Electronics ships faster HBM4E chip samples to customers; shares jump,” May 28, 2026.  
https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/samsung-electronics-ships-hbm4e-chip-samples-global-customers-2026-05-28/

A Korean supply-chain / internal-briefing report translated by Jukan says Samsung CTO Song Jai Hyuk told the DS division that HBM4E reliability-test yield has moved above 70%, and that D1d process development is targeting PRA in November.

Model treatment:

- Not used to mechanically raise FY2028 operating profit.

# 8. Why Ceiling Is Unchanged

The HBM4E / D1d report is a strong positive probability update, but it does not yet disclose:

- customer qualification status;
- production purchase orders;
- HBM4E contracted volumes;
- customer mix;
- average selling price;
- gross margin by HBM generation;
- D1d mass-production yield;
- HBM5 customer qualification.

Therefore the right treatment is:

> Raise Base / confidence, not the FY2028 ceiling.

Ceiling can be raised if HBM4E volume shipments or D1d / HBM5 data prove stronger than the current DS Memory model.

# 12. Key Risks

1. **HBM4E yield is not company-confirmed** — the 70% figure is a Korean internal-briefing / supply-chain report.
2. **Customer qualification still matters** — yield is not the same as volume shipments.
3. **SK Hynix remains the HBM quality leader today**.
4. **Foundry optionality is not foundry profit yet**.
5. **Samsung is a conglomerate** — MX, display, Harman and other lines dilute the clean memory re-rate.
6. **Korean governance discount remains** — 15x OP is used intentionally.
7. **China competition is long-term** — CXMT is not a 2026–2027 cycle killer, but remains a structural competitor.
8. **Memory-efficiency innovation risk** — extreme DRAM/HBM prices create incentives to reduce memory per token.
9. **HBM5 thermal / packaging complexity** — D1d and HPB-style thermal design must convert into yield and customer adoption.
10. **Currency / Korea market discount** — KRW and Korean equity discount can suppress multiple expansion.
