## RULE 10 VIOLATION: Path A (GOES Monopoly) Inside Path B (Cyclical Steel)

## The Two Companies Inside CLF

**Path A — GOES (Grain-Oriented Electrical Steel):**
Cleveland-Cliffs is the **SOLE US producer** of grain-oriented electrical steel. GOES is the core material for transformer cores — every distribution transformer, every power transformer needs GOES. The DPA explicitly names "electrical core steel." Distribution transformers just added to Section 232 tariff derivatives (50%). The Butler project is expanding GOES capacity. If GOES were a standalone company, it would score **70+ WL** as a physics-constrained domestic monopoly with government-mandated demand.

**Path B — Everything Else ($18B+ Cyclical Steel):**
CLF is a $19B revenue steel company focused on HRC flat-rolled products for automotive (29%), distributors (31%), and infrastructure (29%). It lost $1.43B in FY2025. Net loss of $229M in Q1 2026. HRC prices ($980/ton) determine 90%+ of the P&L. Stelco acquisition (Canada) faces 50% tariffs. POSCO deal uncertain. The stock trades on the steel cycle, not on GOES.

**The Rule 10 problem:** GOES is <5% of CLF's revenue. The monopoly is real but INVISIBLE to the market. The stock moves ±20% on HRC price swings that have nothing to do with transformers. Buying CLF for GOES is buying $18B of cyclical steel exposure to get $500M of monopoly exposure.
