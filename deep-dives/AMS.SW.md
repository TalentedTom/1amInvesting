## Thesis

ams-OSRAM is developing MicroLED arrays for AI datacenter optical interconnects. Goldman Sachs highlights MicroLED as a growing alternative to SiPh CW lasers due to higher energy efficiency, better thermal management, and lower latency — addressing the power wall in Rubin Ultra-era networking. Company has signed an unnamed hyperscaler/AI partner for datacenter optical interconnects with launch targeted for 2027. Demonstrated MicroLED arrays transitioning from proof-of-concept to full product development. TrendForce (May 2026) includes ams-OSRAM in emerging MicroLED CPO supply chain alongside Avicena, AUO/Ennostar, Credo (Hyperlume), and Microsoft (MOSAIC).

## Why It FAILS

1. RULE 16d: Timeline 24-36+ months. "Meaningful ramp H2 2028" = 27 months out. Capped at 65 WL max under timing filter.

2. NICHE TAM: MicroLED CPO TAM $900M by 2030. Goldman ELS TAM for CW lasers = $10,830M. MicroLED is <10% of the light source market even at 2030 maturity. At 25% capture = $225M revenue → $34M NI → $850M MC. Cannot justify current $2.23B MC.

3. FINANCIAL DISTRESS: Massive debt from OSRAM acquisition. Core automotive/display business under pressure. Restructuring 2+ years. This is a struggling conglomerate, not a focused CPO play.

4. "ALTERNATIVE" NOT "STANDARD": MicroLED complements SiPh for low-power edge cases. SiPh CW lasers remain the standard for high-bandwidth CPO. Goldman: SiPh supply "very tight" through 2028 = SiPh IS the market.

5. ALL PRODUCTS IN DEVELOPMENT: Micro lens/emitter arrays (in development), ASIC (early stages), substrate (early stages), advanced packaging (early stages). Nothing shipping. Multiple sequential "IFs" required.
