## Supercycle: AI Datacenter Connectivity — Copper + Optics + Memory Fabric

## Core Thesis: Hybrid AI Interconnect Platform

Credo is no longer just the “purple cable” AEC story. The current thesis is broader:

**Credo is a hybrid AI interconnect platform: ZeroFlap AECs where copper wins, optical DSP / ZeroFlap optics / DustPhotonics silicon-photonics PICs where optics wins, and Weaver / OmniConnect where inference memory bandwidth becomes the next bottleneck.**

The AI network is becoming hybrid rather than single-architecture:

- **Short-reach, latency-critical scale-up:** copper / AEC / retimers remain strong because they avoid E/O conversion, minimize latency, and are serviceable.
- **Rack-to-rack scale-out:** optics wins where reach, power-per-bit, and cable density dominate.
- **NPO/CPO transition:** value moves closer to switch ASICs and optical engines, but the link still needs SerDes, DSP/analog, PICs, telemetry, and reliability.
- **Memory-fabric / inference architectures:** large-context and agentic inference increase the value of moving data between compute and memory efficiently.

Credo sits at the seam of those transitions. That is valuable because the industry is not choosing copper **or** optics. It is deploying copper where latency and cost dominate, optics where reach and density dominate, and eventually memory/interconnect products where inference economics demand new fabric primitives.

The framework classification remains **not a pure chokepoint**. Credo has a strong qualification and product-stack moat, but Broadcom, Marvell, Astera Labs, Semtech, Montage, and internal hyperscaler silicon can contest parts of the stack. The score reflects platform breadth and execution, not monopoly physics.

## Why AECs Still Matter

The “copper is dead” call remains premature.

In short-reach, latency-critical domains — inside racks, adjacent racks, and tightly coupled AI pods — copper/AEC can win on latency, reliability, power, serviceability, and cost. A single unstable link can delay cluster bring-up or reduce GPU utilization. Credo’s ZeroFlap positioning is not just a cable slogan; it is a reliability and utilization argument.

But AECs have a distance ceiling. Once the domain moves rack-to-rack across broader data-center fabrics, optics dominates. That is why the current thesis is not “copper beats optics.” It is:

**Credo wins if copper lasts in short-reach scale-up long enough for the optical and memory-connectivity legs to scale.**

## Cross-Book Read-Throughs

**SMTC:** Semtech validates the same copper/optics seam from a different product mix: FiberEdge, CopperEdge, and HieFo. CRDO is higher-beta and purer; SMTC is broader and less pure.

**ALAB:** Complement and competitor. ALAB binds tightly clustered GPUs into platform fabrics; CRDO links servers/racks and increasingly participates in optics. Taurus/AEC and retimer competition are real.

**MRVL / AVGO:** Platform competitors and ecosystem validators. If custom ASICs and optical fabrics fragment, both can win at system level while CRDO wins link-level content.

**AAOI:** Mixed. CRDO’s optical ramp validates optical demand, but also shows value moving into DSP/PIC/reliability, not just finished modules.

**SIVE / LITE / COHR / AXTI / AIXA / AEHR:** Positive indirect read. More optical/PIC demand increases demand for lasers, InP substrates, MOCVD, and test.

**PENG / RMBS / memory-logistics theme:** Weaver/OmniConnect and CXL retiming fit the broader agentic-inference memory bottleneck. PENG is the AI factory/memory orchestration layer; CRDO is one potential physical link layer.

## Key Risks

1. **Customer concentration:** Q4 FY2026 had four 10% customers at 34%, 27%, 16%, and 10% — roughly 87% of revenue combined. Any deployment delay, architecture change, or digestion at a major customer can move the stock and the model.
2. **H2 FY2027 optical ramp execution:** FY27 guide relies on optical contributing >$600M and stronger second-half growth. A slip in ZeroFlap optics, DustPhotonics PICs, or optical DSP ramps would reset expectations.
3. **Copper distance ceiling:** AECs remain strong in short-reach scale-up, but cannot replace optics at scale-out / scale-across distances.
4. **Competitive sockets:** AVGO, MRVL, ALAB, SMTC, Montage, and internal hyperscaler silicon contest pieces of SerDes, DSP, retiming, AEC, and optical engines.
5. **DustPhotonics integration:** Strategic acquisition, but PIC roadmap and NPO/CPO revenue must convert; integration risk is real.
6. **Weaver still a breadcrumb:** $2,000–$3,000 per GPU is interesting but not proven. Current model appropriately probability-weights it at 50%.
7. **Optical price compression:** If optical costs fall faster than expected, the relative advantage of copper can narrow in some links even as CRDO’s optical portfolio offsets.
