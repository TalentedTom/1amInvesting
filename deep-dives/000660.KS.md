## Supercycle: DB6 Memory Structural

## Core Thesis: The HBM Leader Becomes NVIDIA's Co-Design Partner

SK Hynix is #1 in the binding constraint of AI compute: 57% HBM share, ~70% of NVIDIA Vera Rubin HBM4 orders (UBS), and a 4nm logic base die Samsung has not matched — a 12-18 month lead that compounds each generation. Ford Principle: not "memory chips" — AI intelligence capacity. Memory bandwidth IS the transformer bottleneck; no HBM, no AI.

**The structural re-rating anchor (Jun 2026): NVIDIA co-design partnership.** SK Hynix memory teams are jointly designing across Vera Rubin, Vera CPU, RTX Spark, and Jetson Thor, inside an EDA triangle with NVIDIA — the "memory as co-design partner, like TSMC" relationship. A co-design seat is not a vendor slot: it cannot be bid away on price, it embeds SKH in architecture decisions a generation early, and it is the strongest single argument that this cycle's economics are structural.

**Super-Supplier behavior:** Alphabet, Microsoft, and Meta offered to fund the Yongin Y1 fab (₩31T) and subsidize EUV (₩12T). SK Hynix **refused** — accepting would create exclusive-supply obligations diluting pricing power. Choosing independence over free capital is monopoly behavior. The same demand pressure shows up as 3-5 year LTA-only sales (Microsoft, Google) with supplier-floor pricing, and management on record: "customer demand for HBM over the next three years already far exceeds our production capacity"; Chairman Chey: wafer shortage "likely to persist until 2030," shortfall exceeding 20%.

**Why 2026 ≠ 2018:** then smartphone-cyclical, spot-priced, no LTAs, -60% in six months; now AI-structural, 3-5yr LTAs with floors, hyperscaler capex rising, Apple absorbing 3x memory pricing with a $400 MacBook pass-through. New demand layers not in prior models: CPU DRAM going 4x (300-400GB per AI CPU), SOCAMM $8→$13/GB, 10% structural DRAM deficit, IDC's "potentially permanent strategic reallocation" of wafer capacity.
