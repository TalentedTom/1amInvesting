## Thesis

Jabil is a $35B/year global electronics manufacturing services (EMS) company undergoing a rapid AI-driven mix shift. The Intelligent Infrastructure segment (~47% of FY2026 revenue, growing ~34% YoY) now dominates the growth profile, encompassing server/rack integration, networking equipment, silicon photonics modules, liquid cooling, power distribution, and capital equipment. FY2026 AI-related revenue of ~$13.6B represents 50% YoY growth.

The company operates as a system-level integrator for hyperscalers, offering compute, networking, power, and cooling as a holistic capability rather than siloed products. Dastoor (CEO, Q3 FY2026 call): the third hyperscaler has been won, with initial revenue in the "couple of hundred million dollar range for '27, expanding to $1 billion and beyond in '28." A strategic alliance with Adani Enterprises aims to establish an AI and data center infrastructure manufacturing platform in India, with "fiscal 2028 the more realistic starting point for meaningful contributions."

### Why the Framework Scores Jabil at 68 WL

Jabil is a well-run, rapidly growing company riding the AI supercycle — but it fails the framework's core tests for bottleneck positioning.

**Rule 9 (disappearance test):** If Jabil disappeared tomorrow, could the AI infrastructure build continue? Yes. Celestica, Flex, Foxconn/FII, Quanta, and Wistron all compete in the same segments. Hyperscalers would redistribute production across other qualified EMS providers. Capacity would take 6-12 months to replicate, not years.

**Rule 6 (competitive vs monopoly):** Jabil competes with multiple capable players in every segment. In server/rack integration: Celestica, Flex, Foxconn. In networking equipment: same set plus ODMs. In silicon photonics module assembly: they acquired Intel's SiPh manufacturing capability, but foundry wafers come from TSEM, lasers from SIVE/LITE/COHR — Jabil is the assembler, not the bottleneck component. In power/cooling: multiple capable integrators exist.

**Rule 10 (Path A inside Path B):** The AI infrastructure growth engine (~47% of revenue, accelerating) sits inside a diversified $35B conglomerate with automotive, healthcare, renewables, and consumer segments. The non-AI segments dilute the growth thesis and compress the achievable multiple.

Jabil is ecosystem beta (Rule 9), not bottleneck alpha. It is a high-quality Layer 2 (module/subsystem) player in the signal hierarchy. The framework owns Layer 3 and below.

## Key Risks (each scored)

1. **Competitive replication (-1):** No moat prevents CLS/FLEX/FII from offering identical capabilities. Jabil's system integration advantage is execution-dependent, not IP-protected. Customer switching costs are moderate (qualification + ramp time) but not structural.

2. **Customer concentration (-1):** Three hyperscalers driving the Intelligent Infrastructure thesis. If any hyperscaler insources or shifts to a competitor, the growth profile compresses materially. The Adani alliance adds geographic diversification but introduces India execution risk.

3. **Margin sustainability (-1):** The 6%→7%+ OP margin trajectory requires sustained mix improvement, new facility ramp execution, and continued AI demand outpacing supply. EMS margins are structurally capped by the contract manufacturing model — Jabil does not own the IP, design, or critical components. New facility startup costs (NC, India) create near-term drag.

## Re-Rate Triggers

1. **FY2027 guidance (September 2026):** CEO signaling well above 6% margin and "similar" AI growth rates. If formal guide confirms $40B+ revenue at 6.2%+ OP, validates the model. COUNTER: guide below $38B or margin guidance flat at 6% = thesis weakens.

2. **Third hyperscaler revenue disclosure:** Initial revenue expected "couple hundred million FY27, $1B+ FY28." Revenue run-rate confirmation = validates V1 scaling. COUNTER: delays or scope reduction.

3. **Adani alliance crystallization (FY28):** Currently "intent to form strategic alliance." Joint venture formation, facility groundbreaking, or customer commitments = de-risks the India vector. COUNTER: Adani governance/regulatory risk.

4. **Margin accretion from SiPh/power/cooling:** Dastoor positioned these as accretive to II margins. Sustained II margin above 6.5% for two consecutive quarters = mix thesis validated.

5. **Multiple re-rate:** If the market begins valuing Jabil's AI infrastructure segment separately (sum-of-parts), the conglomerate discount narrows. CLS comps at 17-20x would imply significant re-rate. COUNTER: continued non-AI segment drag keeps multiple compressed.

6. **MONOPOLY FALSIFICATION:** Framework score depends on the conclusion that Jabil is NOT a bottleneck. If Jabil demonstrates sole-source positioning in any critical SiPh/CPO assembly step (e.g., exclusive tooling, process IP), Core Thesis re-examines upward from Rule 10 cap. Currently no evidence supports this.
