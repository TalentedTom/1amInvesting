## Supercycle: Memory Structural (DB6) + China Semiconductor Materials (国产替代)

## Core Thesis: The Memory Capex Cycle's Consumables Annuity

NAURA captures the equipment capex first. Yoke captures the recurring materials annuity after that capex turns into wafer starts. Equipment is one-time; materials consumption is every wafer, every quarter, for the life of the fab. Once a precursor or specialty chemical is qualified into a memory line, the fab keeps consuming it — the qualification wall (2-3 years) creates the stickiness, and the capex cycle creates the demand base.

Yoke is a Chinese semiconductor materials platform company that acquired Korean precursor maker UP Chemical, giving it 18% global ALD/CVD precursor market share (#3 behind Merck and Shin-Etsu, three companies control >80% of high-end market). The crown jewel: SK Hynix HBM4 dielectric-layer precursor SOLE SUPPLIER, orders locked to 2031. Also exclusive molybdenum (Mo) precursor supplier for the W→Mo transition in HBM4/5 interconnects. 7N purity (99.99999%). Additionally supplies Samsung (20-25%), Micron, TSMC, Intel, SMIC, CXMT (>60% domestic precursor share), and YMTC.

The capex-to-materials bridge (from NAURA artifact, China fab capex ¥1,537→¥3,900亿 FY2025-2028): CXMT's disclosed 2025 raw-material procurement was ~¥114.7亿, with chemicals at 37.29%, photoresist at 12.16%, and specialty gas at 5.10%. These map directly onto Yoke's product set. As CXMT/YMTC/SMIC add 5-6万 wafers/month in 2026-2027, ALL materials consumption scales proportionally — and unlike equipment, the consumption is RECURRING.

**Moat type:** CERTIFICATION WALL (2-3 year qualification cycle) + OLIGOPOLY (3 companies dominate high-end precursors) + CUSTOMER LOCK-IN + REGULATORY (国产替代 for domestic fabs). Management confirmed: "high-end precursors have high barriers, strong customer stickiness, and stable pricing."

**Classification:** ⚡ BOTTLENECK — HBM precursor position is real but clears when Merck/Shin-Etsu expand capacity or new domestic competitors qualify (3-5yr timeline). Not a chokepoint because the chemistry is replicable given time. However, the MATERIALS ANNUITY quality (recurring consumption proportional to wafer throughput) is structurally superior to one-time equipment capture.

## KEY RISKS (scored)

1. **Goodwill impairment (-1):** ¥21.13亿 from UP Chemical acquisition = 22% of net assets. Annual impairment testing required. If UP Chemical underperforms or Korea-China trade tensions disrupt, significant write-down is possible.

2. **Customer concentration (-1):** SK Hynix (overseas) and CXMT (domestic) are disproportionately important. Single customer reduction = material revenue impact. ~40% of total revenue from overseas storage customers.

3. **Photoresist GM drag (-1):** Photoresist/supporting reagents was ¥19.60亿 in FY2025 at only 16.72% GM — the weakest margin in the platform. As this segment grows to ¥47亿 by FY2028, low GM drags the blended margin unless product mix shifts to higher-end (advanced packaging, glass substrate). This is the single biggest margin risk.

4. **WF6 hype / ordinary precursor competition (-1):** Stock spiked Jun 10-11 on false WF6 narrative — company officially clarified it has NO tungsten hexafluoride business. Specialty gas is mainly CF4/SF6, only 5.79% of FY2025 revenue. Ordinary silicon precursors face domestic competition and price volatility. High-end precursors are differentiated; low-end are not. Valuation at ~50x forward P/E reflects concept premium, not fundamentals.

**Unscored risks (monitor):**
- Geopolitical: Korea-China trade restrictions could impair UP Chemical (Korean subsidiary) operations
- A-share regime: concept-driven volatility, ¥188.60 already prices significant growth
- Raw material inflation: high-purity hafnium, molybdenum supply constrained
