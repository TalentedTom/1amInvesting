## Core Thesis: The Glass Science Monopoly Buried Inside a Conglomerate

Corning owns three genuinely monopoly-grade glass technologies that are critical to the AI infrastructure buildout: (1) Glass Bridge — IOX waveguide technology for fiber-to-PIC coupling in CPO, (2) Glass Core — glass substrates for semiconductor packaging (CoPoS competitor), and (3) CPO FlexConnect — bend-resilient fiber optimized for inside-the-box CPO deployments. Combined with its existing #1 position in optical fiber globally, Corning IS the physical-layer glass infrastructure company for AI data centers.

BUT — these monopoly technologies sit inside a $190B conglomerate that also makes display glass (flat YoY), automotive glass (-1% YoY), Gorilla Glass, solar panels (volatile), and life sciences equipment. **Rule 10 applies hard:** the Optical Communications segment, if spun off, would score 80+ as a pure-play AI optical infrastructure company with NVIDIA backing and $18B+ in hyperscaler LTAs. Buying the holdco dilutes the AI thesis with non-AI drag segments.

## Rule 6 Test

"If Corning disappeared, could AI optical infrastructure still work?" 

**PARTIALLY.** Optical fiber: OFS/Furukawa, Sumitomo, Prysmian can supply. FAUs: FOCI and others. CPO FlexConnect specialty fiber: NO clear substitute — Corning's bend-resilient, short-length fiber is unique for inside-the-box CPO. Glass Bridge IOX waveguides: NO substitute — this is genuinely unique glass science that only Corning can do. Glass Core substrates: SKC Absolics, SCHOTT compete.

The honest answer: Corning passes Rule 6 on Glass Bridge and CPO FlexConnect (genuinely irreplaceable), but fails on commodity fiber and standard FAUs (many competitors). It's a MIXED result — monopoly on the emerging technologies, ecosystem player on the established ones.
