## Core Thesis: Contracted Neocloud at SPAC Pricing

Boost Run is a neocloud — GPU compute, CPU nodes, managed Kubernetes, shared storage — listed via SPAC on Nasdaq May 11, 2026, holding NVIDIA **Exemplar Cloud** status on Blackwell (the validated-provider tier) and Preferred Cloud Partner standing. The setup: **$1.41B of non-cancelable contracted backlog against a ~$2.2B market cap** — roughly two-thirds of the company's value already under signed three-year contracts — at ~6x FY2028 earnings with 27% modeled net margins. Either the market is wrong (a discovery gap born of SPAC stigma and three weeks of trading history), or there is execution risk the contracted numbers don't capture. The position of this artifact: watch the first verification quarters before deciding which.

Explicitly NOT a bottleneck: cloud capacity is replicable, the moat is contracts + NVIDIA validation + compliance certifications (SOC 2 Type II, HIPAA, ISO 27001/27701), not physics or patents. BRUN sits on the **paying side** of every chokepoint the core book owns — it is a customer of the supercycle, included for the valuation asymmetry, not the framework fit.

## Key Risks

1. No technology moat — when contracts expire, customers can move; the moat must be re-signed annually
2. SPAC mechanics — warrant dilution schedule undisclosed in detail; institutional skepticism structural
3. Customer concentration — TML at 33% of backlog is single-customer risk in a startup customer
4. Execution on a 10x ramp — revenue recognizes only as capacity deploys; delays are revenue delays
5. Dell single-supplier dependency — $1.44B through one hardware channel
6. Layer squeezability — neoclouds sit between hyperscaler-grade demand and supplier-side pricing power (the book's chokepoints are BRUN's costs); margin durability at 27% is the model's boldest assumption
