## Supercycle: MLCC Materials — Rule 10 (Path A inside Path B)

## Core Thesis: ~25% Global MLCC Dielectric Powder Share, Buried in a Diversified Chemical Conglomerate

Sakai Chemical is the world's second-largest supplier of MLCC dielectric powder (barium titanate), with ~25% global share behind Sinocera (~30%). Founded 1918, TSE Prime listed. Vertically integrated from raw material procurement (via subsidiary Sakai Shoji) through to finished high-purity powder.

The MLCC powder thesis is real: every capacity expansion at Murata, Taiyo Yuden, SEMCO, or any MLCC maker requires Sakai Chemical's barium titanate. AI server MLCC demand growing 80%+ CAGR. Murata raised AI server MLCC prices +25% in Q2 2026.

**However: Rule 10 applies.** Electronic Materials is one of 11 business segments. The company also sells titanium dioxide, cosmetics raw materials, cold medicine ("Kaigen"), resin additives, catalysts, and medical devices. The MLCC chokepoint is a Path A monopoly embedded inside a Path B industrial conglomerate. The stock trades on the overall chemical business, not purely on MLCC powder dynamics.

## Key Risks

1. **Rule 10 — Path A inside Path B.** MLCC powder is real but 80% of the business is unrelated industrial chemicals. Stock will trade on TiO2 prices and pharma revenue most of the time.
2. **FY3/2024 loss year.** -¥7.09B NI demonstrates earnings volatility from non-MLCC segments.
3. **Sinocera gaining share.** 30% vs 25% — the Chinese competitor is growing faster with government backing.
4. **Japanese market discount.** TSE-listed Japanese chemicals companies trade at structurally lower multiples than Chinese or US peers.
5. **Revenue CAGR ~3-5%.** This is not a hyper-growth story. The overall company grows slowly; only the MLCC segment accelerates.
6. **¥5.7B capex plan.** Large investment relative to current OP. Execution risk on high-end MLCC capacity buildout.

## Why This Ranks Low vs CPO Names

The fundamental problem: Sakai Chemical has a real 25% share in a critical MLCC material, but you can't buy the chokepoint without buying the cold medicine, the cosmetics, and the titanium dioxide. Your CPO positions (SIVE, FOCI, ShunSin) are pure-play bottleneck companies where 80-100% of revenue comes from the thesis. Here, 80% of revenue dilutes the thesis.

The stock jumped 16.5% on May 29 because the market suddenly noticed the MLCC connection. But that kind of catalyst-driven re-rating is one-off, not structural. The stock will revert to trading on overall chemical earnings within weeks.

Watchlist for MLCC sector intelligence. Not a portfolio candidate unless the Electronic Materials segment is spun off or becomes majority of revenue.
