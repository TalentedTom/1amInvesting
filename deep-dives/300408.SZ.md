## Supercycle: DB1 Photonics (connector precision components — ferrule layer) + DB-MLCC (materials/passive components)

## The Screening Question, Answered — Two Theses, One Company

The dive entered on the MLCC thesis (Jukan's "longest shortage in history") but the research revealed a **different and stronger thesis hiding beneath it**: Three-Circle holds one of the most concentrated monopolies in the entire optical networking stack, and almost nobody in the English-speaking investment world knows about it.

**Thesis 1 (the steak): Fiber Optic Ceramic Ferrule Monopoly**
Three-Circle holds **70-80% global market share in fiber optic ceramic ferrules** (国家制造业单项冠军 = National Manufacturing Single Champion designation), the precision ceramic alignment sleeves inside every LC, SC, and fiber optic connector. More critically: **MT/MPO multi-core ferrules at 85%+ global market share** — monopoly-grade dominance in the exact high-density connector standard that 800G and 1.6T data center interconnects require. Every optical module from AAOI, Innolight, LITE, COHR, and every transceiver on earth terminates in a connector with a ceramic ferrule, and Three-Circle makes 70-85% of them. Ferrule insertion loss determines signal quality at the physical layer. The moat: sub-micron ceramic alignment precision, decades of zirconia/alumina processing know-how, multi-year qualification cycles, and 90%+ self-manufactured equipment (competitors cannot replicate the production line without building it from scratch).

**Thesis 2 (the sizzle): Vertically Integrated MLCC with Full Powder Self-Supply**
Three-Circle is the fastest-growing domestic Chinese MLCC producer: Q1 2026 revenue ¥2.68B (+46%), NI ¥791M (+48%). Already batch-supplying AI servers with high-capacity MLCCs (sub-1μm dielectric layers, >1,000-layer stacking). Global MLCC share only ~2% (tiny vs Murata's 40%), but the competitive moat is vertical integration: **100% barium titanate powder self-supply and >90% equipment self-manufactured** — the AAOI model applied to passive components. In the "longest MLCC shortage in history," self-supplying your key input while competitors source from Sakai/Sinocera is a genuine advantage. Goldman Sachs (May-Jun 2026) published three deep-dive reports calling MLCC "the next major supply bottleneck after GPU and HBM."

**Additional monopoly positions (the ceramic platform):**
- Alumina ceramic substrates: **50% global market share**
- Ceramic packaging bases: **~40% global market share** (serving semiconductor packaging — the DB5 chain)
- SOFC (solid oxide fuel cell) membrane: **#1 globally** (the carbon-neutral optionality)

## Rule 10 Assessment — NOT capped

Unlike Sakai (MLCC materials = ~20% inside a chemical conglomerate selling cold medicine and cosmetics), Three-Circle's revenue splits as: electronic elements/MLCC ~42% + optical communications/ferrules ~36% + ceramics/SOFC/other ~22%. **Both major segments (~78% combined) serve the AI infrastructure stack directly.** The remaining ~22% includes ceramic substrates (50% global, semiconductor-relevant) and SOFC (clean-energy optionality). This is NOT a conglomerate hiding a gem — it's a dual-thesis company where both theses ride the same supercycle.

## Cross-Book Relevance — the Missing Connector Layer

The book maps the optical chain from lasers (SIVE, LITE, COHR) through foundries (Win Semi, TSEM) to packaging (ShunSin, FOCI) and test (AEHR). **The connector layer was unmapped until this dive.** Every laser chip, every PIC, every transceiver module, every CPO package — they all terminate in fiber optic connectors that use Three-Circle's ceramic ferrules at 70-85% global share. Three-Circle is to the connector what SIVE is to the laser: the invisible monopoly producing the precision component without which the optical signal cannot physically couple from fiber to chip.

Specifically: FOCI's FAU (fiber array unit) work involves aligning fibers to chips — the ferrule is the upstream precision component that determines whether that alignment holds. AAOI's transceivers plug into connectors with Three-Circle ferrules. LITE's modules, COHR's modules — same. Three-Circle's pricing and availability affects every downstream optical position in the book.

The Sakai/Sinocera pair now has a MLCC-chain counterpart: **Sinocera makes the ceramic powder → Sakai makes the powder → Three-Circle makes finished MLCCs from its own powder AND makes the ferrules that connect the optical modules.**

## Key Risks

1. **Chinese A-share governance and regulatory environment** — foreign investor access via Stock Connect only (H-share filing is the de-risking catalyst)
2. **MLCC global competition** — 2% global share vs Murata's 40%; the MLCC thesis is growth, not monopoly
3. **Reverse export-control risk** — if China weaponizes ceramic ferrule exports (the InP precedent in reverse), Three-Circle becomes a geopolitical tool rather than a beneficiary. The company is currently a beneficiary of Chinese sovereignty policy; it could become a victim of Western counter-policy
4. **Valuation** — at ¥105 ATH, the stock trades ~60x trailing earnings; the MLCC mania may have front-loaded the discovery
5. **RMB currency risk** for USD/CAD-based investors
6. **Concentration** — the ferrule monopoly means most revenue comes from a single product category that could face ceramic-alternative disruption (polymer ferrules exist at low-end; irrelevant for high-speed but watch)
