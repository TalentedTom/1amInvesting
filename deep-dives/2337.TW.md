## Supercycle Position: Memory Supercycle (Legacy Flash — The Accidental Monopoly)

## Core Thesis: "Raise Prices However You Want"

The Big Four memory giants (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron, Kioxia) are abandoning legacy NOR flash, SLC NAND, and eMMC to chase AI-driven HBM and advanced NAND — inadvertently creating a near-monopoly for Macronix in these "legacy" segments. Samsung announced June 2026 as the final shipment date for MLC eMMC. Macronix President Chih-Yuan Lu told investors the eMMC market "could be dominated by Macronix alone." His comment on pricing: "The current situation is basically 'raise prices however you want.'"

This is the AI crowding-out effect in action. The same supercycle that drives SK Hynix's 72% operating margin on HBM is ALSO creating scarcity in legacy memory by pulling capacity, materials, packaging, and test resources away from older products.

## The AI "Butterfly Effect" on Legacy Memory

Martin Lin (Macronix head of marketing) describes a chain reaction:

1. AI demand absorbs wafer fabrication capacity → less capacity for legacy nodes
2. AI demand absorbs packaging/test capacity → legacy memory lead times extend
3. Big Four exit legacy products to maximize AI margins → supply shrinks further
4. NOR flash demand IN AI servers INCREASES (30+ devices per rack, up from 3-5)
5. Result: legacy memory prices spike, Macronix becomes sole supplier in multiple segments

## NOR Flash in AI Servers — The Surprise Demand Vector

NOR flash per AI server rack: increased from ~3-5 devices to **30+ devices.** Every AI server needs NOR flash for firmware storage, boot sequences, and configuration management. As AI racks get more complex (more GPUs, more networking, more management controllers), NOR flash content per rack increases.

NVIDIA's Rubin rack offloading HBM storage burden to NAND SSDs (Jensen Huang, CES 2026) creates ADDITIONAL demand for flash — and the packaging/test capacity for that flash competes with the same resources Macronix needs.

## The "Accidental Monopoly" Question

The framework treats monopolies differently based on DURABILITY:

| Type | Example | Score Premium |
|------|---------|------|
| Physics monopoly | SIVE (CW laser), AEHR (WLBI) | Maximum — can't be replicated |
| IP/patent monopoly | SOI.PA (Smart Cut 4,300 patents) | High — decades of protection |
| **Market-exit monopoly** | **Macronix (Big Four leaving)** | **Moderate — competitors CAN return** |
| Capacity monopoly | TSEM (SiPh foundry share) | Moderate — others expanding |

Macronix's monopoly exists because Samsung/SK Hynix/Micron/Kioxia CHOSE to exit legacy segments. They could CHOOSE to re-enter if Macronix raises prices too aggressively. This is a rational economic decision by competitors, not a physics barrier. The monopoly is durable while AI absorbs their capacity — but if AI spending pauses, legacy capacity returns.

Framework distinction: SIVE's monopoly exists because NO ONE ELSE CAN make CW lasers at production quality (physics barrier). Macronix's monopoly exists because no one else WANTS TO make legacy eMMC right now (economic choice). The first is permanent. The second is conditional.
