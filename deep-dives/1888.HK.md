## Supercycle: PCB Materials — CCL Shortage at "Crisis Level"

## What Kingboard Does

Kingboard Laminates is the GLOBAL LEADER in copper clad laminate (CCL) manufacturing — the foundational substrate material for ALL printed circuit boards. CCL is a panel made by laminating thin copper foil onto an insulating substrate (fiberglass + resin). Every electronic device on earth contains PCBs made from CCL.

```
VERTICAL INTEGRATION (Kingboard's genuine moat):

Unlike competitors who BUY raw materials:
  Kingboard PRODUCES its own:
    → Fiberglass yarn ← produces internally
    → Glass fabric ← produces internally  
    → Copper foil ← produces internally
    → Epoxy resin ← produces internally
    → CCL (finished product) ← sells to PCB makers

  When input prices spike:
    Competitors: Input cost ↑ → margin ↓ → price hike → lag
    Kingboard: Input cost ↑ → captures margin on BOTH input AND CCL
    
  When input prices fall:
    Competitors: Input cost ↓ → margin recovery
    Kingboard: Internal supply cushions against price volatility
    
  This is a genuine competitive advantage — but it's COST advantage,
  not TECHNOLOGY advantage. Any competitor with $2B+ could replicate.
```

**Production expansion:** CNY 2B ($260M) investment at Shixing Industrial Park in Guangdong for electronic-grade fiberglass fabric and electronic yarn. New lines starting mid-2026.

## Why The Shortage Is STRUCTURAL, Not Cyclical

```
THREE SIMULTANEOUS DRIVERS (from Macro_Lin):

1. HIGH-END CROWDING-OUT:
   1 unit high-end CCL capacity displaces 4-5 units standard capacity
   Producers converting standard lines → AI-grade (higher margins)
   AI shortage mechanically creates standard CCL shortage
   Same pattern as HBM conversion draining conventional DRAM
   
2. EQUIPMENT BOTTLENECK BEHIND CAPACITY BOTTLENECK:
   CCL production equipment booked through Q1 2028
   Lead times: 8 months → 2 years
   You can't expand capacity without machines
   Machines have a 2-year queue
   Hard floor on supply tightness through 2027 minimum

3. M9 INFLECTION (H2 2026):
   M8: current volume for Blackwell
   M9: required for GB300 52-layer boards (H2 2026)
   M10: Rubin architecture (samples shipping, volume H2 2027)
   
   Each grade upgrade:
     → Fewer qualified suppliers
     → Higher unit cost
     → Lower volume capacity
     → More complex resin systems (hydrocarbon replacing epoxy)
     → Tighter supply
```
