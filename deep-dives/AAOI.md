## 1. Investment thesis and supercycle position

AAOI provides an investable exposure to the shift toward higher-speed optical modules through its US manufacturing footprint, internal laser capability, and flexible 800G/1.6T assembly lines. The investment thesis requires qualified output and a richer product mix to convert a large market into company revenue. It is a temporal manufacturing and qualification bottleneck, rather than a demonstrated monopoly in standardized transceivers.

The operative S-curve is an 800G ramp followed by a faster 1.6T mix shift. External light-source modules for CPO provide another revenue vector, subject to customer qualification, high-power laser capacity and packaging yield. A laser consumed inside an AAOI transceiver is an input cost; it is not a second external revenue sale.

The model pools high-speed module capacity, reaches 90% saleable utilization in Q4 2027, and shifts 1.6T to 60% of high-speed units at that point and 80% by Q4 2028. It holds total high-speed capacity at 930,000 units per month during 2028. That is a finite modeling assumption, not a company statement that expansion stops. Pricing declines eventually outrun mix improvement at that capacity level.

The 3.2T industry ramp belongs in the active research horizon. AAOI-specific qualification, orders, testing throughput and a production schedule are not established in the materials reviewed, so no 3.2T revenue is included in the current projection. Higher market volume alone does not grant AAOI a constant market share or remove component bottlenecks.

This makes shipment-weighted annual ASP equal the stated benchmark. The within-year curve is an explicit interpolation assumption; GS did not provide these AAOI quarterly prices. ## 11. Risks and falsification tests

The five risk classes below correspond to the five one-point deductions in the Base table.

1. **Execution and component supply (-1).** The projection requires new capacity to become qualified output, utilization to rise, and product allocation to shift toward 1.6T. Final testing, DSP/TIA supply and laser production can constrain output despite nominal assembly capacity. Counter: material production delay or persistent failure to convert installed capacity into recognized shipments. A Q4 2026 revenue result below $400M would trigger a full thesis review; this is a research threshold, not an instruction to trade.
2. **Competition, concentration and price/cost economics (-1).** Customer concentration and standardized module competition limit pricing power. The GS benchmarks may differ from AAOI's SKU mix; preserving projected margins requires the displayed cost curve. Counter: realized ASP, yield or cost evidence that invalidates the modeled product margins, or customer allocation loss. High-speed price decreases without corresponding cost improvement directly undermine the profit model.
3. **ELSFP performance, yield and timing (-1).** High-power optical performance, wavelength control, external-module qualification and packaging yield may delay recognition. The model assumes nominal capacity reaches 400K/month by Q4 2027, earlier than the public 2028 wording, and a 38% conversion rate is not a disclosed absolute yield. Counter: an announced delay beyond 2028 or no recognized ELSFP revenue by Q4 2027; revise the vector and all affected four-quarter windows.
4. **Funding and dilution (-1).** Factory expansion and working capital can require equity or other financing. Share counts in the ceiling path are assumptions after the near-term company guide; operating profit is not free cash flow. Counter: share issuance materially above the period-specific schedule or a funding plan that changes capacity timing. Recompute ceilings using the actual diluted-share bridge.
5. **Legacy demand and architecture transition (-1).** Memory-related switch constraints can suppress 100G demand, while a faster architecture transition can displace 800G before AAOI captures the next product generation. Counter: persistent legacy weakness, lost design wins or customer adoption of a product AAOI cannot supply on the modeled schedule.

No sixth risk point is added for the same economics under another label. Supplier agreements, LTAs and geopolitical sourcing developments feed the relevant class above. A broader rescore requires new scoring evidence; this update retains the explicit 43/60 total.
