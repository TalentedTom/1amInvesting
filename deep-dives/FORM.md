## Supercycle: DB1 Photonics Test + HBM Test

## Core Thesis: Probe Cards for the AI Era

FormFactor makes probe cards — the precision tools that test semiconductor wafers before they're cut and packaged. MEMS microsprings contact every die on the wafer and verify electrical functionality. The company is the global market leader in probe cards for DRAM (including HBM), foundry & logic (including 2nm), and is expanding into co-packaged optics testing.

## The "Three Pillars" Growth Strategy

### 1. HBM4 Testing (Near-term catalyst)
- HBM stacks moving from 8-high to 12-high to 16-high dies
- Test complexity increases EXPONENTIALLY with stack height
- FormFactor's MEMS microsprings are "industry standard" for HBM testing
- DRAM probe card revenue: record in Q4, another record guided for Q1
- HBM4 mass production (late 2026) expected to be major earnings inflection

### 2. 2nm Logic Probing
- TSMC and Intel moving to 2nm nodes
- Test points on wafer are closer together than ever
- Requires next-gen probe card technology
- Foundry & Logic segment expanding with GPU and custom ASIC wins

### 3. Co-Packaged Optics Testing (via Keystone Photonics)
- Keystone Photonics acquired December 2025 (~$20M)
- Integrated electrical + optical probe solutions for CPO
- Addresses the SiPh wafer testing bottleneck Serenity identified
- "Strengthened leadership in co-packaged optics testing"

### Bonus: Quantum Cryogenics
- Flatiron Dilution Refrigerator launched March 2026
- "Desktop Quantum" testing at millikelvin temperatures
- Speculative but positions for future quantum computing market

## The PI Hexapod Insight (Framework-Critical)

```
FormFactor makes the probe CARD
  └── Mounted on a prober (FormFactor or MPI)
      └── Using PI (Physik Instrumente) hexapods for 20-50nm alignment
          └── PI = quasi-monopoly in precision alignment (PRIVATE)
              └── THE actual chokepoint is PI, not FormFactor
```

Both FormFactor AND MPI depend on PI's piezo-flexure hexapods for nanometer-precision alignment. The real bottleneck in wafer probing isn't the probe card — it's the alignment subsystem underneath. PI is private and uninvestable, which means the framework cannot capture the actual chokepoint in this chain.
