## Core Thesis: COHR IS the InP Bottleneck

When the market says "InP is short," it is describing Coherent's production lines. COHR owns the most advanced InP laser fabs globally and is running the **world's first 6-inch InP production at two sites simultaneously** (Sherman, Texas + Järfälla, Sweden; Switzerland adding) — with initial 6-inch yields already exceeding mature 3-inch lines. Six-inch wafers carry **2.25x the die of 4-inch**: a structural cost advantage over every competitor still on smaller formats, including LITE. Capacity is doubling in 2026 AND 2027 (>2x cumulative, twice in ~18 months, bookings into CY2028) — and management still calls demand "unprecedented." NVIDIA's $2B investment (Mar 2, same day as LITE's) with a multiyear AI-photonics supply pact validates both as strategic; together they're NVIDIA's locked CPO laser supply, leaving SIVE the independent CW source for everyone else.

Vertical breadth no one matches: substrates → epitaxy → lasers (EML/CW/pump) → transceivers (800G/1.6T) → OCS → CPO → thermal (Thermadite) → SiC. All three Goldman TAM layers simultaneously: EML pluggable $82.8B, CW CPO $70.9B, OCS $39.8B — a $70B+ SAM by 2030 (OFC). InP heading 44%→91% of high-speed lanes by 2030 (LightCounting); the MOCVD demand signal runs through AIXA ("Lumentums and Coherents of the world are buying those machines").
