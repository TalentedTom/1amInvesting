This is the FIRST thing the framework demands clarity on.

## The Margin Problem (Framework-Critical)

Revenue +82% but GM compressed from 26% → 16%. This is the framework's primary concern.

When revenue doubles and margins HALVE, it typically means:
1. Incremental revenue is lower-value services work (final testing, not proprietary burn-in boards)
2. Pricing power is limited — customers dictate terms
3. Scale isn't driving operating leverage
4. The business is selling CAPACITY, not TECHNOLOGY

Net income: $126K on $15.6M revenue = **0.8% net margin.** This is a rounding error. The business is essentially breakeven despite an 82% revenue surge.

For the framework to rate this highly, we'd need to see:
- GM recovering toward 25%+ (proving pricing power)
- Operating income scaling with revenue (proving operating leverage)
- Proprietary technology creating switching costs (proving moat)

Right now we see revenue growth without profitability inflection. That's a VOLUME story, not a MOAT story.

## Why The Framework Scores TRT Low Despite Revenue Surge

The framework is designed to find MONOPOLIES and BOTTLENECKS. TRT is neither.

- AEHR (86): Only company on earth doing WLBI. If AEHR disappeared, entire SiC and AI chip qualification would stop. Physics-required monopoly.
- TRT (52): One of dozens of SE Asian test houses. If TRT disappeared, customers would shift to ASE, JCET, Amkor, Unisem, UTAC, or any other OSAT. Competitive services business.

Revenue growth proves AI test demand is real. That's valuable intelligence for AEHR's thesis. But the framework allocates capital to monopolies, not competitive volume businesses.
