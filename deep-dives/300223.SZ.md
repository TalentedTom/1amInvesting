## 1. Supercycle, supply chain and durable advantage

Ingenic owns ISSI and combines specialty DRAM, Flash/SRAM, embedded processors and analog/connectivity products. Long qualification cycles and long-lived industrial sockets support retention, while large suppliers moving capacity can create temporary pricing power. AI infrastructure adds demand for NOR and memory beside optical/control chips; this is distinct from selling HBM. The current S-curve is a specialty-memory repricing and product-mix ramp. New stacked DRAM and AI MCUs provide later optionality, but are not treated as booked sales. ISSI is part of the group, not an external competitor.

DB6 memory and DB10 edge intelligence: industrial/automotive systems, servers and embedded AI → qualified memory, compute and analog sockets → Ingenic/ISSI design and customer support → external wafer, packaging and KGD supply. Long qualification periods and lifecycle support slow substitution, but alternate qualified suppliers exist. ISSI is an owned business; treating it as a rival would double-count the competitive set. The company benefits from wafer allocation changes at large memory vendors without selling their HBM product. No numerical automotive monopoly share is assumed.

## 8. Multiple, shares and recognition convention

20× forward core operating profit is retained for a qualified specialty-memory and embedded-compute supplier. Customer retention and new applications support a growth multiple; outsourced capacity and cyclical pricing limit the case for a premium scarcity multiple. This is an OP ceiling convention, not an equity-market P/E observation or a claim that automotive scarcity is permanent.

The August return reports 514.951667M combined A/H issued shares. Add 1.792934M potential restricted shares and up to 4.693M overallotment shares to obtain 521.437601M, conditional on full issuance. No buyback reduction is assumed. A/H shares represent claims on the same consolidated operating stream; the denominator is not A shares alone. The August 25 listing is already an event, while potential additional allotment still requires reconciliation. Minor non-controlling interests do not materially change H1 net earnings; future subsidiary ownership, cash, borrowings and commitments must be checked before treating this OP multiple as a common-equity cash valuation.

The valuation denominator is the ceiling quarter’s diluted-share proxy. It is not a historical EPS weighted-average assumption disguised as a fully known future capitalization. Unit factor: 1; this converts the model units to CNY per share. No cash balance, enterprise-value adjustment or after-tax earnings substitution is added to the operating-profit ceiling.
